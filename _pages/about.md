    <h2 id="research-title">Research</h2>
  </div>

  <div class="research-intro-layout">
    <div class="home-prose home-research__intro">
      <p>Much of my research lies in the triangle spanned by algebraic topology, combinatorial topology, and dynamics. I am particularly interested in how these perspectives interact, from quantitative homotopy invariants and finite models to Morse theory and fixed-point phenomena.</p>
    </div>

  <div class="home-prose home-research__profile-link">
    <p>Throughout this page I highlight a selection of my work. For a complete list of publications, see my <a href="https://zbmath.org/authors/mosquera-lois.david">zbMATH profile</a>.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="research-strand" aria-labelledby="algebraic-combinatorial-title">
    <h3 id="algebraic-combinatorial-title">Algebraic &amp; Combinatorial Topology</h3>

    <div class="research-editorial-row">
      <div class="home-prose research-editorial-row__text">
        <p>I am interested in quantitative homotopy invariants measuring the complexity of spaces and the homotopical difference between maps. Homotopic distance provides a common framework for classical invariants such as Lusternik–Schnirelmann category and topological complexity. This viewpoint was introduced in <a href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematical-proceedings-of-the-cambridge-philosophical-society/article/homotopic-distance-between-maps/295660946813AD8BC9FB410346FB0B98"><em>Homotopic distance between maps</em></a> (<em>Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society</em>, 2022), with my PhD advisor Enrique Macías-Virgós.</p>
      </div>

      <figure class="research-illustration">
        <img src="{{ '/images/research-homotopic-distance.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Diagram illustrating homotopic distance between two maps" width="1448" height="1086" loading="lazy">
      </figure>
    </div>

    <div class="research-editorial-row research-editorial-row--reverse">
      <figure class="research-illustration research-illustration--wide">
        <img src="{{ '/images/research-simplicial-distance.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Simplicial complexes illustrating contiguity distance" width="1578" height="683" loading="lazy">
      </figure>

      <div class="home-prose research-editorial-row__text">
        <p>I am particularly interested in approaching these invariants through algebraic and combinatorial methods. In the preprint <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18356"><em>A Computational (Co)homological Approach to Contiguity Distance</em></a> (2025), with my PhD student Ángel Méndez-Vázquez and Enrique Macías-Virgós, we develop computable cohomological methods for contiguity-type distances.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="research-editorial-row">
      <div class="home-prose research-editorial-row__text">
        <p>This also leads me to finite spaces and categorical models of topology. In the preprint <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18650"><em>Weak and Strong Fibrations of Functors</em></a> (2026), with my former PhD student Isaac Carcacía-Campos and Enrique Macías-Virgós, we extend fibration, sectional-category and motion-planning ideas to small categories.</p>

        <p>I am also interested in finite models in their own right. In the preprint <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06176"><em>On Whitehead's theorem for minimal finite models</em></a> (2026), I give a negative answer to an open question of Barmak concerning the validity of Whitehead's theorem for minimal finite models.</p>
      </div>

      <figure class="research-illustration">
        <img src="{{ '/images/research-finite-models.png' | relative_url }}" alt="A topological space shown alongside a finite combinatorial model" width="1448" height="1086" loading="lazy">
      </figure>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="research-strand" aria-labelledby="topology-dynamics-title">
    <h3 id="topology-dynamics-title">Topology &amp; Dynamics</h3>

    <div class="home-prose research-strand__lead">
      <p>A second strand of my research explores how topological methods can encode dynamical information, particularly through Morse theory and Lefschetz fixed-point theory.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="research-editorial-row">
      <div class="home-prose research-editorial-row__text">
        <p>In <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00009-022-02166-4"><em>Homotopic Distance and Generalized Motion Planning</em></a> (<em>Mediterranean Journal of Mathematics</em>, 2022), with Enrique Macías-Virgós and María José Pereira-Sáez, we use Morse–Bott theory to relate homotopic distance to generalized motion-planning problems.</p>
      </div>

      <figure class="research-illustration research-illustration--wide">
        <img src="{{ '/images/research-motion-planning.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Diagram illustrating generalized motion planning between two spaces" width="2100" height="749" loading="lazy">
      </figure>
    </div>

    <div class="research-editorial-row research-editorial-row--reverse">
      <figure class="research-illustration research-illustration--shallow">
        <img src="{{ '/images/research-morse-bott.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Morse–Bott diagram relating topological and fixed-point information" width="1109" height="236" loading="lazy">
      </figure>

      <div class="home-prose research-editorial-row__text">
        <p>I am especially interested in bringing Morse theory and Lefschetz theory closer together. In <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s13398-026-01858-w"><em>Morse–Bott Inequalities for Endomorphisms</em></a> (<em>RACSAM</em>, 2026), with my former PhD student Alejandro O. Majadas-Moure and my PhD advisors Enrique Macías-Virgós and José Antonio Vilches, we develop inequalities that bring together the classical Morse inequalities and Lefschetz fixed-point theory, yielding information that neither framework provides on its own.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="home-prose research-standalone">
      <p>With my former PhD student Alejandro O. Majadas-Moure, in <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11784-025-01204-4"><em>Integration with respect to the Lefschetz number</em></a> (<em>Journal of Fixed Point Theory and Applications</em>, 2025), we develop an integration theory based on the Lefschetz number, with applications including sensor-based object detection.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="research-editorial-row research-editorial-row--last">
      <div class="home-prose research-editorial-row__text">
        <p>Finally, I am interested in how fixed-point theory behaves in finite and combinatorial settings. In <a href="https://www.math.ubbcluj.ro/~nodeacj/volumes/2026-No2/262-mou-vir-loi-24-0039_finalizat.php"><em>Lefschetz fixed-object and fixed-morphism theorems for acyclic categories</em></a> (<em>Fixed Point Theory</em>, 2026), with Samuel Castelo-Mourelle and Enrique Macías-Virgós, we construct Lefschetz-type invariants detecting fixed objects and fixed morphisms of endofunctors of finite acyclic categories.</p>
      </div>

      </figure>
    </div>
  </div>
